Estimation of Chlorophyll-a Concentrations in Small Water Bodies: Comparison of Fused Gaofen-6 and Sentinel-2 Sensors

Authors: Jiarui Shi, Qian Shen, Yue Yao, Junsheng Li, Fu Chen, Ru Wang, Wenting Xu, Zuoyan Gao, Libing Wang, Yuting Zhou
Journal: Remote Sensing, 2022, 14, 229

Abstract & Purpose
Chlorophyll-a (Chla) concentration is a key indicator for water quality and eutrophication.
Monitoring Chla in small water bodies (streams, ponds, small lakes) is challenging due to their narrow, fragmented nature and the limited spatial resolution of most free satellite sensors.
This study fuses Gaofen-6 images to obtain 2 m resolution, 8-band images and compares their performance to Sentinel-2 (10 m, 13 bands) for Chla estimation.
Five semi-empirical and four machine learning models are compared using simulated reflectance.
The extreme gradient boosting tree (BST) model is the most accurate:
For Sentinel-2: MRE = 9.03%, RMSE = 4.5 mg/m³
For fused Gaofen-6: MRE = 6.73%, RMSE = 3.26 mg/m³
Fused Gaofen-6 offers higher spatial resolution; Sentinel-2 offers higher temporal resolution.
Study Area & Data
Location: Beijing, China (lat 39.4°N–41.6°N, long 115.7°E–117.4°E)
Water bodies: Jima River, Yongding River, North Canal, Chaobai River, Ji Canal, Miyun Reservoir, Guanting Reservoir, Huairou Reservoir, Haizi Reservoir, and several urban ponds.
Field data: 82 water body spectra from six field surveys (2016–2019) at various sites.
Satellite data:
Gaofen-6 PMS (2 m, 4 bands: blue, green, red, NIR)
Gaofen-6 WFV (16 m, 8 bands: aerosol, blue, green, yellow, red, red-edge I, red-edge II, NIR)
Sentinel-2 MSI (10–60 m, 13 bands: aerosol, blue, green, red, red-edge I/II/III, NIR, narrow NIR, water vapor, SWIR, cirrus)
Methods
Field Measurements
Remote sensing reflectance (Rrs):
Rrs(lambda) = [Lu(lambda) - rsky(lambda) * Lsky(lambda)] / [Lp(lambda) * pi / rho_p(lambda)]
Lu(lambda): upwelling radiance above water
rsky(lambda): Fresnel reflection coefficient
Lsky(lambda): sky radiance
Lp(lambda): radiance of reference panel
rho_p(lambda): reflectance of reference panel (~30%)
Spectral simulation for satellite bands:
Rrs(lambda_i) = [integral from lambda_min to lambda_max of Rrs(lambda) * f(lambda) d(lambda)] / [integral from lambda_min to lambda_max of f(lambda) d(lambda)]
f(lambda): relative spectral response function of the band
Chlorophyll-a measurement:
Water filtered, frozen, extracted in hot ethanol, acidified, and absorbance measured at 665 and 750 nm.
Image Processing
Fused Gaofen-6:
Image fusion (PAN + MSS), geometric correction (Long’s method), atmospheric correction (relative radiation normalization using Sentinel-2 as reference).
After correction, 7 bands used: blue (450–520 nm), green (520–590 nm), red (630–690 nm), NIR (770–890 nm), red-edge I (690–730 nm), red-edge II (730–770 nm), coastal aerosol (400–450 nm).
Sentinel-2:
Atmospheric correction using Sen2Cor.
Accuracy Metrics
Mean Relative Error (MRE):
MRE = (1/n) * sum over n of |Ri - Rj| / Rj * 100%
Ri: estimated Chla
Rj: in situ Chla
n: number of samples
Root Mean Square Error (RMSE):
RMSE = sqrt( sum over n of (Ri - Rj)^2 / n )
Modeling Approaches
Semi-empirical models:
Used Rrs(675 nm)/Rrs(705 nm) as main independent variable.
Five model forms: linear, exponential, logarithmic, power, quadratic.
Example formulas for fused Gaofen-6:
Linear: Chla = -65.788453 * x + 99.5654
Exponential: Chla = 1485.8 * exp(-3.65197 * x)
Logarithmic: Chla = 37.9778 * x^4.0432
Power: Chla = -83.5964 * log(x) + 35.9646
Quadratic: Chla = 186.537 * x^2 - 511.3751 * x + 362.781
Example formulas for Sentinel-2:
Linear: Chla = -83.028 * x + 107.0218
Exponential: Chla = 1139.618 * exp(-3.98529 * x)
Logarithmic: Chla = 20.76 * x^3.6926
Power: Chla = -85.2146 * log(x) + 23.37083
Quadratic: Chla = 119.624 * x^2 - 485.476 * x + 306.563
Machine learning models:
Multiple Linear Regression (LR), Bayesian Regression (BR), Extreme Gradient Boosting Tree (BST), Artificial Neural Network (ANN).
Input features: combinations of 7 reflectance bands, red-edge/red ratios, and red/red-edge ratios (up to 9 features).
Best performance with 9 inputs (7 bands + 2 ratios).
Results
Semi-Empirical Model Performance
Fused Gaofen-6:
Quadratic model best: MRE = 11.9%, RMSE = 6.04 mg/m³
Sentinel-2:
Quadratic model best: MRE = 10.64%, RMSE = 5.75 mg/m³
Machine Learning Model Performance
Fused Gaofen-6:
BST model best: MRE = 8.76%, RMSE = 3.36 mg/m³ (validation set)
Sentinel-2:
BST model best: MRE = 11.78%, RMSE = 5.75 mg/m³ (validation set)
Image-Based Validation
BST model on images:
Fused Gaofen-6: MRE = 6.73%, RMSE = 3.26 mg/m³
Sentinel-2: MRE = 9.03%, RMSE = 4.5 mg/m³
Quadratic function model on images:
MRE and RMSE > 50% and 28 mg/m³ (not suitable for image-based estimation)
Comparison in Ponds and Rivers
Fused Gaofen-6 provides more detailed spatial information, especially in small ponds and narrow rivers (<10 m wide), where Sentinel-2 cannot resolve water bodies.
Both sensors provide consistent Chla estimates in larger water bodies, with TMRE (total mean relative error) below 15%.
Temporal Coverage
Fused Gaofen-6: variable, meets quarterly observation frequency.
Sentinel-2: fixed orbit, monthly coverage.
Joint use increases monitoring frequency for small water bodies.

Conclusions
Both fused Gaofen-6 and Sentinel-2 can estimate Chla in small water bodies, with the BST machine learning model providing the best accuracy.
Fused Gaofen-6 is superior for spatial detail (2 m), enabling monitoring of smaller/narrower water bodies.
Sentinel-2 is superior for temporal frequency (monthly).
Joint use of both sensors is recommended for comprehensive monitoring.
