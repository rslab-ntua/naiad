Title: Remote sensing of inland Sabkha and a study of the salinity and temporal stability for sustainable development: A case study from the West coast of Qatar

Key Points:

Objective: Map minerals, rocks, salt crusts, gypsiferous soil flats, and study soil salinity and temporal stability of the Dukhan inland sabkha (salt flat) in Qatar using remote sensing and field/lab validation.
Motivation: Sabkha areas are important for infrastructure and agriculture but are challenging due to high salinity, unstable soils, and aggressive environments. Remote sensing offers a way to monitor and assess these areas for sustainable development.
Study Area: Dukhan sabkha, west coast of Qatar, 73 km2, largest inland sabkha in the Arabian Gulf. Characterized by arid climate, little rainfall, and complex geology (Rus and Dammam Formations, Quaternary deposits).
Data and Methods:

Satellite Data: Used Hyperion (EO-1), ASTER (Terra), and Sentinel-2 MSI.
Mineral Mapping: Hyperion data processed with Linear Spectral Unmixing (LSU) to map evaporite minerals (halite, gypsum, anhydrite, calcite, dolomite).
Rock Discrimination: ASTER VNIR and SWIR bands processed with Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to distinguish geological formations.
Soil Salinity Mapping: Sentinel-2 MSI monthly data (2018-2020) analyzed using Normalized Difference Salinity Index (NDSI), formula:
NDSI = (band11 - band12) / (band11 + band12)
Soil Moisture Mapping: Sentinel-2 MSI data analyzed using Normalized Difference Moisture Index (NDMI), formula:
NDMI = (band8 - band11) / (band8 + band11)
Field and Lab Validation: Fieldwork (2019-2020) to verify satellite results, collect samples, and measure reflectance spectra, mineralogy (XRD), salinity, electrical conductivity (EC), total dissolved solids (TDS), and pH.
Results:

Spectral Absorptions: Gypsum, anhydrite, and halite show unique absorptions at 1.4-1.5 microns and 1.9-2.0 microns. Calcite and dolomite show deep absorptions near 2.345 and 2.495 microns.
Mineral Distribution: LSU mapping shows halite over salt crusts, gypsum over gypsiferous flats, calcite and dolomite over carbonate rocks.
Lithological Discrimination: PCA of ASTER bands distinguishes evaporite crusts, gypsiferous flats, and carbonate formations.
Soil Salinity: NDSI maps show high salinity in gypsiferous sand and silt flats and salt crusts, especially from April to August (hot, dry months). Salinity is lower in winter months.
Soil Moisture: NDMI maps show higher moisture in evaporite crusts and gypsiferous flats during summer, lower in carbonate rocks. Moisture and salinity patterns are dynamic and linked to climate.
Field and Lab Validation: Fieldwork confirms mineral and soil distributions. Lab measurements show very high salinity (up to 116.8 ppt), EC (up to 175.5 mS/cm), and TDS (up to 141.9 g/L) in salt crusts and brines. Soils are loose, unstable, and cemented by salts.
Conclusions:

Remote sensing with Hyperion, ASTER, and Sentinel-2 MSI, combined with field and lab validation, effectively maps minerals, salinity, and moisture in inland sabkha.
The sabkha environment is highly saline, dynamic, and aggressive, posing challenges for sustainable development.
NDSI and NDMI indices from Sentinel-2 MSI are effective for monitoring salinity and moisture changes over time.
Results are valuable for planners, engineers, and environmental managers considering infrastructure or agriculture in sabkha regions.
Formulas:

Normalized Difference Salinity Index (NDSI): NDSI = (band11 - band12) / (band11 + band12)
Normalized Difference Moisture Index (NDMI): NDMI = (band8 - band11) / (band8 + band11)
