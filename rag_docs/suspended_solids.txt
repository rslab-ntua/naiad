Estimating Total Suspended Solids (TSS) in Inland and Coastal Waters from Sentinel-2 MSI: A Semi-Analytical Approach

Key Points

Objective: Develop and validate a semi-analytical method to estimate TSS from Sentinel-2 MSI imagery, applicable to a wide range of water types (clear to extremely turbid) and suitable for small inland/coastal water bodies.
Motivation: Most global lakes are too small for medium-resolution sensors (MERIS, OLCI, MODIS). Sentinel-2 MSI offers higher spatial resolution (10–20 m), enabling monitoring of small lakes, rivers, estuaries, and nearshore waters.
Data: 1265 in situ Rrs-TSS pairs from 69 lakes and 11 coastal waters worldwide. Synthetic datasets for algorithm calibration and validation. 17 Sentinel-2 MSI images and 314 MSI images for time-series analysis. 674 Sentinel-3 OLCI images for comparison.
Atmospheric Correction: Used C2RCC processor in SNAP for MSI and OLCI images.
Method Overview

Estimate Rrs(620) from Rrs(665):
MSI lacks a 620 nm band. Rrs(620) is estimated from Rrs(665) using a third-order polynomial:
Rrs(620) = 169.385 * Rrs(665)^3 - 15.576 * Rrs(665)^2 + 1.317 * Rrs(665) + 0.000148
Water Type Classification:
Waters are classified into four types (clear, moderately turbid, highly turbid, extremely turbid) using Rrs at 490, 560, 620, and 740 nm.
Estimate bbp (particulate backscattering coefficient):
Use the Quasi-Analytical Algorithm (QAA) to estimate bbp at a reference band, which varies by water type.
Reference bands: 560 nm (clear), 665 nm (moderately turbid), 740 nm (highly turbid), 865 nm (extremely turbid).
Estimate TSS:
TSS = 1 / (b_bp(lambda) * bbp(lambda))
b_bp values are optimized for MSI bands using synthetic data.
Validation and Results

Validation datasets: Synthetic data, in situ data, and 40 satellite matchups.
Performance:
Median Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE): Synthetic: 14.88 percent, In situ: 31.50 percent, Satellite matchups: 41.69 percent
The method outperforms existing semi-analytical (Nechad et al. 2010), empirical, and machine learning (MDN, SOLID) methods, especially in clear and extremely turbid waters.
MSI-derived TSS is generally closer to in situ TSS than OLCI-derived TSS, especially in small or spatially heterogeneous water bodies.
Advantages of MSI

High spatial resolution enables monitoring of small lakes, rivers, estuaries, and nearshore waters, which are not observable with medium-resolution sensors.
More detailed TSS maps: MSI captures fine-scale spatial variations, such as river plumes, sediment resuspension, and anthropogenic features (for example, bridge effects).
Collaborative use with OLCI: OLCI provides higher temporal frequency, MSI provides higher spatial detail.
Limitations and Recommendations

Atmospheric correction accuracy is critical; errors in Rrs propagate to TSS estimates.
Water type misclassification at boundaries can cause minor errors, but the method is robust to small uncertainties in Rrs(620).
Users are encouraged to use local or in situ b*_bp values if available for improved accuracy.
Conclusions

The proposed semi-analytical method enables accurate, high-resolution TSS mapping from Sentinel-2 MSI for a wide range of water types.
MSI is particularly valuable for monitoring small and dynamic inland/coastal waters.
The method is robust, outperforms existing approaches, and is suitable for long-term and event-based TSS monitoring.
Both MSI and OLCI can be used collaboratively for comprehensive TSS monitoring.
