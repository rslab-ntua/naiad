Cleaned Version: Simultaneous Retrieval of Water Quality Indicators from Landsat-8, Sentinel-2, and Sentinel-3
Overview
This work presents a machine learning approach (Mixture Density Networks, MDNs) for retrieving three key water quality (WQ) indicators from satellite data:

Chlorophyll-a (Chla)
Total Suspended Solids (TSS)
Absorption by Colored Dissolved Organic Matter at 440 nm (acdom(440))
The method is designed to work across data from three major satellite missions: Landsat-8 (OLI), Sentinel-2 (MSI), and Sentinel-3 (OLCI). The goal is to create consistent, global water quality products from multi-source satellite imagery.

Key Points
Challenge: Different satellites have varying sensor characteristics, making it hard to create unified water quality products.
Solution: Use a machine learning model (MDN) trained on a large, diverse set of in situ measurements to predict Chla, TSS, and acdom(440) from satellite reflectance data.
Performance: The MDN model outperforms traditional algorithms, especially for TSS and acdom(440), and provides consistent results across different satellites within known uncertainty ranges.
Data: The model is trained on a global dataset of paired satellite reflectance and in situ water quality measurements, with data from over 40 sources worldwide.
Handling Missing Data: The model uses multiple imputation to handle missing values in the training data, improving robustness.
Validation: Two main validation methods are used:
Hold-out: Randomly splits data into training and testing sets.
Leave-one-out (LOO): Trains on all but one data source and tests on the excluded source, repeated for all sources.
Uncertainty: The model provides uncertainty estimates for its predictions, which are important for practical use.
How the Model Works
Input: Satellite reflectance data (Rrs) from OLI, MSI, or OLCI sensors.
Output: Simultaneous predictions of Chla, TSS, and acdom(440).
Architecture: Five hidden layers, each with 100 nodes, using five Gaussian mixtures to model output distributions.
Training: Uses a bagging ensemble of ten models for robust predictions.
Results
Accuracy: MDN achieves lower bias and uncertainty than other models, especially for TSS and acdom(440).
Consistency: Products from different satellites are consistent within model uncertainty, especially for TSS and acdom(440). Chla estimates are more accurate from MSI and OLCI than from OLI.
Sensitivity: The model is less sensitive to atmospheric correction uncertainties than other algorithms.
Limitations: Local or regional models trained on specific data may outperform the global model in those areas.
Practical Use
Global Monitoring: The MDN model enables harmonized, global-scale monitoring of water quality indicators from multiple satellite sources.
Local Adaptation: For best results in specific regions, retrain or fine-tune the model with local data.
Uncertainty Awareness: Users should consider the provided uncertainty estimates, especially when comparing results across different sensors or under challenging atmospheric conditions.
