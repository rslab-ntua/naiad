Title: Discharge Estimation Using Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 Product: Case Studies in the Murray Darling Basin

Authors: Zhuolin Shi, Yun Chen, Qihang Liu, Chang Huang

Abstract and Purpose:
This study evaluates the use of the C/M method (ratio of calibration land pixel to river measurement pixel in the NIR band) with the Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 (HLS) surface reflectance product to estimate river discharge in small rivers (30–100 m width) in the Murray-Darling Basin, Australia. The method is validated using two years of data.

Key Concepts and Methods:

The C/M method uses the ratio between a stable land pixel (Calibration, C) and a river pixel (Measurement, M) in the NIR band.
Linear regression is applied between the C/M time series and observed discharge to build a site-specific model.
Data used: HLS surface reflectance product (combines Landsat-8 and Sentinel-2, 30 m resolution, NIR band), observed daily discharge data from two gauges: 410130 (Murrumbidgee River, ~60 m wide) and 414200 (Murray River, ~90 m wide).
2017 water year for modeling, 2018 for validation.
Landsat-8 NIR: Band 5 (845–885 nm). Sentinel-2 NIR: Band 8A (865 nm, resampled to 30 m).
C is the average of multiple stable land pixels (lowest 5% coefficient of variation, CV, in NIR reflectance).
M is the single river pixel with highest Pearson correlation (r) between C/M and observed discharge (p < 0.05).
Region of interest is 31 × 31 pixels (~450 m from gauge).
Formulas (all as plain text):

Coefficient of Variation (CV):
CV = standard deviation / mean

Root Mean Square Error (RMSE):
RMSE = sqrt( sum over t=1 to n of (Qrs_t - Qg_t)^2 / n )

Relative Root Mean Square Error (RRMSE):
RRMSE = (RMSE / average Qg) * 100%

Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE):
NSE = 1 - ( sum over t=1 to T of (Qg_t - Qrs_t)^2 ) / ( sum over t=1 to T of (Qg_t - average Qg)^2 )

Where:
Qrs or Qrs_t = remotely sensed estimated discharge (at time t)
Qg or Qg_t = observed discharge (at time t)
average Qg = mean of observed discharge
n = number of observations
T = total number of time steps

Results:

Gauge 410130 (~60 m): RMSE = 4.61 m³/s, RRMSE = 53%, NSE = 0.24 (moderate accuracy).
Gauge 414200 (~90 m): RMSE = 20.87 m³/s, RRMSE = 19%, NSE = 0.69 (good accuracy).
The method works better for wider rivers with higher base flow.
The model built on 2017 data was validated on 2018 data.
The C/M method with HLS data can estimate discharge in small rivers, especially when river width is sufficient for the 30 m pixel size.
Limitations and Considerations:

The C/M method requires a time series of observed discharge for model calibration.
Cloud cover can reduce the number of usable optical images.
The method is less accurate for very small rivers or those with significant sediment changes.
Resampling Sentinel-2 NIR from 10 m to 30 m may reduce sensitivity.
The method's accuracy may vary with river morphology.
Conclusions:

The HLS-based C/M method is effective for estimating discharge in small rivers (30–100 m width).
Blending multisource NIR data (Landsat-8 and Sentinel-2) increases temporal resolution and enables more frequent discharge estimation.
The approach is promising for ungauged or data-scarce regions, provided a similar gauged basin exists for model transfer.
